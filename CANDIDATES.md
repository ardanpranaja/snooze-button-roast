# Candidates

Alternate lines considered for "Snooze Button Roast". The chosen line won because the punchline lands in the last few words and the angle is fresh: the alarm performs the panic of a late commuter while having no job at all.

1. "Saturday, 6 AM: my alarm clock rang like the weekend owed it money." — Tight and punchy, but the punchline is a familiar "owed money" turn and the alarm itself gets no character.
2. "My alarm clock went off at 6 AM on a Saturday, the only employee in this house who works weekends for free." — Good reversal, but "works weekends" is a setup that explains itself; the chosen line hides the twist until the end.
3. "At 6 AM on a Saturday my alarm clock sang like a rooster that got fired from the farm for being too early." — A fun concrete image, but two jokes compete (fired rooster, too early) and the line runs long.
4. "My alarm clock rang at 6 AM on a Saturday, as if my bed had filed a noise complaint against my dreams." — Dreamy and specific, but the alarm is a bystander in its own roast; the punchline is about the bed, not the clock.
5. "My alarm clock rang at 6 AM on a Saturday with the confidence of a man who has never once been snoozed." — Ironic and on-theme, but it leans on the snooze button rather than roasting the 6 AM ringing itself.

Chosen: "At 6 AM on a Saturday, my alarm clock screamed like it was late for a job it doesn't even have."
